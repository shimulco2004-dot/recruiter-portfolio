# Two independent event design studies

Status: Self-Initiated Concept Projects. Both brands, products, venues and events are fictional. Visualizations do not document completed client work. Brand names are creative proposals, not trademark-clearance claims.

## Phase 1 — TAVRIN: the story

**Company.** TAVRIN is an imagined Riyadh financial technology studio exploring calmer ways to interact with everyday money. Its fictional product team combines payment-interface design with wearable hardware. This story asserts no real company history, banking license or payment-network partnership.

**Product.** PULSE 01 is an imagined watch with a circular e-ink-style display, brushed titanium case, deep graphite strap, a simple tactile crown and no borrowed device silhouettes or interface marks. The proposed experience combines a time display, spending snapshots, a user-controlled payment confirmation and a quick privacy lock. These are concept features, not tested functionality or security claims.

**Audience.** Design-aware GCC professionals, technology editors, potential retail partners and product teams. The audience wants useful technology with a restrained appearance and clear controls.

**Launch context.** A proposed evening launch in a fictional contemporary Riyadh conference pavilion: reception, seated reveal, small demonstration stations and a media corner. The event introduces the relationship between the device, its interface and everyday use.

**Objective.** Make a complex financial-technology proposition immediately understandable: the person controls the moment of payment, while the watch keeps the interaction brief.

**Brand personality.** Precise, composed, human, direct. Confident without making financial promises.

**Campaign / event title.** YOUR TIME. YOUR MOVE.

**Key message.** A considered financial interaction, designed around the rhythm of your day.

**Tagline.** Make time for what matters.

**Central idea.** An interval is both a measure of time and a space to make a decision. Three offset bars move through a rigid grid: a quiet visual sequence of pause, confirm, continue. This connects finance, time and human agency without using currency symbols, stock charts or generic neon circuitry.

**Visual concept and strategy.** A cobalt field acts as a recognizable event landmark. White typography and a repeated interval mark establish hierarchy at distance. Editorial, mostly white printed pieces make detailed information easier to read. The typography is a neutral grotesk with tabular numerals and monospaced metadata. Short headlines align to a strong left edge; microtype stays on handheld material, not across the stage.

**Palette.** Signal cobalt #214BFF; ink #111827; ice #EEF3F8; white #FFFFFF; aluminum #B7C0CC. Cobalt is the only saturated accent. No decorative gradients.

**Logo direction.** A custom TAVRIN wordmark paired with three progressively offset rectangular bars. Flat, reproducible in one color, never a credit-card-network or device-company imitation.

**Typography.** Arial for headlines and body; Courier New for times, sequence numbers and functional labels. These portable families keep the editable vector masters consistent. The portfolio presentation uses its existing Manrope interface. Final production needs font packaging or approved outlined text.

**Graphic system.** Three intervals, fixed baseline, fine dividing rules, oversized numerals and ample negative space. The logo and headline never overlap the intervals. Directional arrows are functional, not ornament.

**Photography.** One original product still with consistent circular watch design, neutral aluminum surfaces and soft directional light. Human context, where used, should show natural adult gestures and everyday clothing. No holograms, floating tokens or speculative security diagrams.

**Materials / production.** Matte laminated vinyl, wrapped modular reception counters, tension-fabric backdrop, aluminum framing, replaceable rigid graphics, restrained acrylic wordmarks and diffuse light. Screen content uses RGB and a separate safe area; printed art uses printer-supplied ICC conversion after proofing. Proposed dimensions are intentions, not structural drawings.

## Phase 3 — SEREV: the story

**Brand.** SEREV is an imagined contemporary fashion atelier creating fluid layers and quietly structured garments for life between private and public spaces. It is an independent fictional label, not an extension of an existing fashion house.

**Collection / event.** THE BETWEEN — Collection 01. An intimate Riyadh collection presentation and showroom experience.

**Audience.** Fashion editors, stylists, creative buyers and design-aware adults who value silhouette, movement and material over visible status symbols.

**Fashion philosophy.** Garments should allow the wearer to choose how much structure, ease and presence they bring to a space. Modest proportions and architectural layers are a creative direction, not a claim about all regional fashion.

**Purpose.** Introduce a first fictional collection through a short seated presentation, close garment viewing and tactile printed material. Guests move from a compressed threshold into a quieter open display room.

**Campaign story.** The most revealing moment happens between poses: a coat turning, a sleeve falling, light passing across a seam. The campaign observes these pauses rather than presenting an idealized luxury lifestyle.

**Main concept.** A seam is where two surfaces meet without becoming the same. One off-center seam line and a cropped frame become the identity's organizing device. Unlike TAVRIN's repeated engineered bars, SEREV uses asymmetry, paper texture, fabric and editorial pacing.

**Tagline.** Form, in motion.

**Atmosphere.** Quiet, tactile and intimate. Warm neutral lighting, uncoated paper, oxblood accents, raw brushed metal rails and natural fabric. No gold monograms, marble props or floral luxury clichés.

**Art direction.** Oversized serif titles with small widely spaced sans-serif labels. A warm chalk field and deep oxblood create strong tonal contrast. One terracotta detail is reserved for navigation and collection numbering.

**Palette.** Oxblood #4A1E2A; paper #F3EEE5; carbon #282522; clay #BF6846. Paper is a material reference, not a low-contrast text color.

**Typography.** Georgia for expressive headlines; Arial for practical information. These families are used consistently in the flat artwork. The portfolio page adds Cormorant Garamond for its editorial presentation headings. Final output needs approved fonts or outlines. No tiny letterspaced body copy.

**Photography.** Original editorial imagery of adults in fully clothed sculptural layers: an oxblood long coat and chalk tunic/trouser silhouette. Natural movement, visible weave, calm expressions, a simple plaster studio, soft lateral daylight, no identifiable designer patterns or logos.

**Graphic system.** An off-center vertical seam, a gently interrupted frame, serif collection title, small 01 collection index. The wordmark stays horizontal. Functional signs use clear sans-serif arrows and destinations, not ornamental serif text.

**Materials.** Uncoated cotton-feel stock, one-color printing where practical, deep oxblood dyed paper bags, blind deboss as an optional finishing sample, matte fabric backdrops, removable vinyl, powder-coated rails and washable fabric scrims. Separate digital colors from physical ink samples; inspect proof under venue lighting.

**Environmental direction.** Two fabric planes create an open threshold. Low furniture and a clear central runway keep the garment silhouettes visible. Freestanding display rails sit away from the visitor route. Layout and dimensions remain conceptual until a venue survey.

## Phase 5 — Artwork and mockup rules

Flat SVG artwork is the reference of record. Every asset will have its own prompt/specification in `design-prompts.md`. Generated environmental images are conceptual visualizations. Preserve the exact identity and key wording when applying flat designs. Never use an invented redesign as the final flat asset. Inspect render text, logo proportions, material behavior and viewpoint before selecting it.

Mockup sequence for each brand: (1) wide stage/runway hero; (2) entrance and reception; (3) product/garment display plus wayfinding; (4) photo/media wall; (5) print collateral close-up; (6) digital campaign context. A product still or editorial photograph supports art direction but does not count toward the twelve assets.
